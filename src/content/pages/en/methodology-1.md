<h2 id="summary">Summary</h2>

We list software and digital services from organisations **headquartered and legally incorporated in Switzerland**. Being a Swiss company is the entry ticket — it says nothing yet about where data is stored. That is why every entry answers five questions separately: headquarters, ownership, development, hosting and infrastructure.

A Swiss SaaS company running on a hyperscaler in Frankfurt is listed as a Swiss company, but does _not_ get a “Hosted in Switzerland” badge. A foreign company that merely operates a data centre in Zurich is _not_ a Swiss software provider.

<h2 id="criteria">Who qualifies</h2>

A product is listed when **one** of the following is true:

1. **Swiss operating company.** The organisation that develops and operates the product is headquartered in Switzerland and registered in the Swiss commercial register (or is a Swiss foundation, association, cooperative or public institution).
2. **Swiss-developed open source.** The product is open source and primarily developed in Switzerland, even without a Swiss operating company.
3. **Swiss-hosted foreign service.** A foreign company hosts core customer data in Switzerland by default. Such entries are clearly labelled and never described as Swiss companies.

In addition, the product must be actively offered and available to customers in Switzerland.

### Parent companies and subsidiaries

- A Swiss company that is **acquired by a foreign group** stays listed as long as it remains headquartered in Switzerland and operates the product from there. It is classified as _Swiss company · foreign parent_ and does not receive the “Swiss Owned” badge.
- A **Swiss subsidiary of a foreign corporation** that sells its parent’s products (for example a local sales office) does not qualify — the product is not developed or operated by a Swiss organisation.
- **Resellers** of foreign software do not qualify. Swiss providers that build their own service on foreign cloud infrastructure do qualify — the infrastructure is shown openly.

These rules are enforced by the build: an entry that fits none of the tiers fails validation and is not published.

<h2 id="tiers">Classification</h2>

Every listed product receives exactly one classification, derived automatically from its data.
